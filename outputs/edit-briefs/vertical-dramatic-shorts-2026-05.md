## Edit Style

Cut frequency: Slow to moderate — sections run 4–6 seconds per cut during analytical narration; compress to 2–3 seconds during data-heavy callout moments (budget figures, revenue numbers) to sustain forward momentum without becoming frantic.

Rhythm approach: Follow narration. This is a trade-register channel — cuts should land on the natural end of a clause or sentence, not against the narration for artificial energy. The exception is the Hook's opening 30 seconds, where a single aggressive cut at the Quibi/revenue-graph beat is earned by the argument's logic, not by editorial reflex.

Reference edit style: Bloomberg Originals (YouTube), Wendover Productions. Both share the same discipline: cuts serve information, not stimulation. No jump-cut energy, no rapid-fire montage. Every B-roll choice looks deliberate.

## Caption Style

Font: Clean sans-serif — Inter, Neue Haas Grotesk, or equivalent geometric sans. Weight: Regular for body captions, Medium or SemiBold for highlighted key terms. Size: Readable on a 9:16 mobile screen without crowding — approximately 5–6% of frame height. No drop shadows. No rounded pill backgrounds unless they are very dark navy (matches palette). If using a background strip, use deep charcoal at 80% opacity maximum.

Placement: Bottom-centre, fixed position. Do not use dynamic floating captions — they break the trade-publication register. Word-by-word highlight is acceptable if the tool supports colour-only highlight (no animated bounce or scale pulse).

Colours:
- Primary caption text: Clean white (#FFFFFF or #F5F5F5)
- Key term highlight: Warm amber (#D4870A or #C9780C) — matches the channel's accent colour. Use sparingly: financial figures, platform names, defined industry terms (e.g. "work-for-hire", "new-media waiver", "SAG-AFTRA").
- Do not highlight more than one or two words per sentence — if everything is highlighted, nothing is.

Tool: CapCut auto-captions with manual colour override on highlight words, or SubMagic with custom colour profile. If using Descript, export SRT and apply colour coding manually in CapCut or Premiere. Avoid any tool that adds animated text presets — static only.
