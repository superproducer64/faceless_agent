## Treatment Document Structure

The treatment is written in **present tense, active voice, produced prose** — not bullet points. It reads like the show already exists.

---

# [TITLE]
### Adapted from [Author]'s [Original Title] ([Year])
**Format**: [Limited Series — N Episodes / Feature / etc.]
**Genre**: [Primary] with [Secondary] elements
**Tone**: [2–3 adjective description]
**Comparable titles**: [Comp A] × [Comp B]

---

## Logline
[One sentence. Protagonist + want + obstacle + stakes. Under 40 words.]

## Premise
[3–5 sentences. The expanded logline. What is this show? What does it feel like? What does it promise the audience?]

## Why Now
[1–2 paragraphs. What does this story say to a contemporary audience? What is its cultural resonance in the present moment? Why adapt this source NOW rather than 10 years ago or 10 years hence?]

---

## The World
[2–3 paragraphs of immersive, present-tense world description. We are inside the show. The reader should feel the setting before they read a single character name.]

---

## Characters

### [Lead Name]
[3–5 paragraphs. This is the full character study — who they are, what they want, what they need, where they start and where they end. Written in the prose voice of the show.]

### [Antagonist / Second Lead]
[Same treatment]

### [Key Supporting — 2–3 characters]
[1–2 paragraphs each]

---

## Series Arc (or Film Arc)

### The Season
[1 paragraph: The shape of the whole story. Beginning, middle, end — in prose.]

### Episode Summaries
For each episode, write 1–2 paragraphs:

**Episode 1: "[Title]"**
[Open with a compelling hook sentence. Then describe the episode's story in vivid present-tense prose. End with the cliffhanger question.]

**Episode 2: "[Title]"**
[etc.]

---

## Themes
[2–3 paragraphs. What is this show actually about beneath the plot? What questions does it ask? What does it ultimately argue — or refuse to answer?]

---

## Visual Approach
[1–2 paragraphs synthesizing the world-builder's vision into a brief, evocative production-design statement.]

---

## The Source Material & Adaptation Approach
[1 paragraph on the original work's legacy and status. 1–2 paragraphs on the specific adaptation choices made — what was preserved, what was modernized, and why. This addresses the inevitable "why change it?" question proactively.]

---

## Comparable Productions
[3 brief comp titles with a sentence each explaining the comparison and what this adaptation takes from or distinguishes itself from each.]

## Treatment Quality Standards

The treatment must:
- Read as if written by a confident showrunner who has already made the show in their head
- Use concrete, specific, visual language (not abstract descriptions)
- Make the reader feel something — not just understand the premise
- Avoid bullet points, lists, and development-speak
- Be between 2,500 and 5,000 words depending on format scope
