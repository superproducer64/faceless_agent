## Structural Principles

Before mapping, internalize:

1. **Every episode needs its own engine**: A per-episode want/obstacle/outcome that serves the season arc but also functions as a standalone unit of story.

2. **The 10% rule**: Streaming pilots must hook in the first 10% of runtime. Map what happens in the first 6 minutes of Episode 1.

3. **Cut the Victorian middle**: Classic novels sag in their middles. The adaptation cannot. Every scene must: advance plot, reveal character, or do both. If it only establishes atmosphere, it's a production design note, not a scene.

4. **End episodes on questions, not answers**: The audience should feel compelled to press Next Episode.

5. **Character want drives plot**: In the novel, plot may happen to the protagonist. On screen, the protagonist must want something specific in every scene.

## For Features

### Three-Act Structure

#### Act 1 (first 25%)
- Opening image and world establishment
- Protagonist introduction and want
- Inciting incident
- Act 1 turn — protagonist commits to the journey

#### Act 2A (25–50%)
- Rising action and escalating obstacles
- Midpoint — major shift that raises stakes

#### Act 2B (50–75%)
- Protagonist at their most tested
- Major reversal or revelation
- All-is-lost moment

#### Act 3 (final 25%)
- Climax
- Resolution
- Final image
